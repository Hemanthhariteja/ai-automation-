The four tickets that get lost

What's actually falling through

✂✂ 01

The DM sent at 9:40pm

Instagram and Messenger enquiries get read whenever someone finally checks — this reads them the moment they arrive, through the same pipeline as the web form. No separate inbox to remember.

✂✂ 02

The party of twelve, buried

A private-event enquiry for forty guests looks identical to a "what are your hours" question in a flat inbox. Group size and event type are scored signals here, not guesswork — the big booking surfaces first.

✂✂ 03

The copy-paste reply

Replies are drafted from the restaurant's actual hours, services, and contact info — never an invented price or a promise nobody made.

✂✂ 04

The complaint nobody followed up on

A cold-food complaint at 9pm shouldn't wait for morning. A rule fires the instant it's classified — priority raised, owner notified, a follow-up task logged.

How an enquiry moves through the pass

From "someone typed something" to "here's what to do about it"

01  Intake

Same door, two ways in

The public enquiry form or a Messenger message — both land in the same queue, tagged by source.

02  Read

Classify & extract

Reservation, catering, private event, or delivery — plus whatever details are actually there. Nothing guessed, nothing invented.

03  Score

A number you can explain

Party size, urgency, budget, and event type add up to one 0–100 score — every factor visible, none of it a black box.

04  Route

A rule takes it from here

Escalate, notify the owner, draft a reply, or log a follow-up — automatically, the moment the ticket is scored.
